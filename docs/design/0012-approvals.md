## Summary

- **Today** main holds every approval rule in JS: ten token kinds with a two-minute life, the sites' standing rules, the D12 pause, x402's caps, and four grant files it writes itself. The core signs whatever main asks, as 0002 planned ("Policy moves with the Chromium work").
- **Policy in the core ([Q3](#decided-questions), decided):** the policy moves into the core now, in Stage 1, in steps. Main keeps the prompts (0011's approvals document), the nonces and the intent journal, and stays trusted.
- **Two new logic crates,** built for desktop and mobile alike: `fc-approval` (tokens, grants, standing rules, limits, x402 caps, phone jobs) and `fc-eip1193` (the Ethereum dispatcher, ported from 0011's main JS). Time comes in as a parameter, randomness as an injected RNG.
- **Tokens:** the eleven kinds, each bound to the request in the form the core will sign it. A page kind, a site's `safe-send` among them, also binds the attested page (`PageContext`) and 0011's handle. A typed-data token binds digests of the whole parsed request and of its view as well as its EIP-712 hash, as today's binds the whole payload.
- **Grants and rules:** today's, ported with vectors: the account check, the 32-byte and warned-typed-data exceptions, the transaction rules, D12's pause, the fee check, Radicle's R-6 limit and x402's caps. "Always allow" gains three limits ([Q9](#decided-questions), decided): a rule ends after 30 days, a site's automatic signatures and its automatic sends each go at R-6's rate, and a contract rule covers no more ETH than the send it came from.
- **Enforced, not advised ([Q8](#decided-questions), decided):** from APR-4 the core refuses a page's signature unless it checked a token or a standing rule for that page itself. That stops a main path that skips the check after a token or grant exists. It doesn't stop one that skips the prompt: until the core runs the dispatcher, a token or a grant stands on main's word that the user approved. From APR-7, an Ethereum page's tokens and grants come only from the core's own prompts. That covers a page's Safe owners and executor, through a Safe approval, and its Swarm SOCs, through its grant. Main's own `shell` signatures, with no page behind them, are outside Q8. Where the core doesn't make the signature (a Ledger, Radicle, Swarm uploads), it advises. [What the core enforces](#what-the-core-enforces) lists each.
- **The files:** the core takes over the four permission files where they are, in today's format, with atomic writes, one file at a time: it reads all four from APR-3, and writes each only from that file's move step (APB-2, APB-3, X402-2), until which main stays its only writer. Older builds still read them ([Q10](#decided-questions), decided). A limited rule is stored where an older build doesn't look, and the unlimited rule it replaces is cleared in the same write, so an older build reads neither and prompts. 0013 decides any later move.
- **Transaction building is split:** until APR-5 main builds every transaction, as today. From APR-5 the core prepares a page's send (the gas estimate with its 20% buffer, the fees from its own quote), and main chooses the nonce, has the core sign the transaction with no other field changed, journals it and broadcasts it. The nonce and the journal move with 0013's stores.
- **`fc-eip1193`** runs a page's Ethereum request in the core, and asks main for each prompt with a core-to-main request, `approval.request`, under a prompt id the core makes. Main ties that id to 0011's handle.
- **x402:** the core checks the cap or the `x402-payment` token, builds the authorization itself and signs it. A cap stays keyed on the seller alone, the paid link's origin ([Q11](#decided-questions), decided), with the fixes in [Deviations](#deviations).
- **Phone jobs:** the core makes each job from a checked authorization, for the chrome it is sent to, binds what it signs, and checks the answer. A payment's recipient, amount, data and chain are compared when the transaction is visible, at the answer or later, and the payment is reported unverified until then. It ends unknown after a day, under a clock set back, or at once if its record can't be saved, and never as not sent.
- **Migration:** APR-1 to APR-5, APR-7, X402-1 and PHN-1 in the core (APR-6 deferred); APB-1 to APB-4, X402-2 and PHN-2 in the browser.

## Goals and non-goals

**Goals**
- **One copy of the policy,** in logic crates that desktop, mobile and a later Chromium build share.
- **No signature for a page without a check the core made:** a token or a standing rule, for that page and that request. In Stage 1 the user's decision behind a token or a grant is main's word. From APR-7, for Ethereum, it is the answer to the core's own prompt.
- **Parity:** the same decisions as today's JS, pinned by vectors from it. Every difference is listed in [Deviations](#deviations).
- **The same files,** readable by older builds.
- **One step at a time,** each revertible on its own until the enforcing release.

**Non-goals**
- **A compromised main.** Main stays trusted in Stage 1 (0001): it fills the caller context, shows the prompts and relays the decisions. A compromised main can mint any token.
- **The approval surface.** 0011's approvals document shows the prompts. The core tells main what to show.
- **Nonces and the intent journal** (`wallet-intents.jsonl`). They stay in main until 0013 moves the stores ([Transaction building](#transaction-building)).
- **Ledger and Safe in the core:** 0016. Until then main drives the Ledger and builds each SafeTx. The core advises on a Ledger's signature, and checks a Safe's vault owners and executor against the approval their token gave ([A page's Safe and Swarm signatures](#a-pages-safe-and-swarm-signatures)).
- **`swarm-feeds.json` and `swarm-manifest-grants.json`:** 0015 takes the Swarm stores. Until then the manifest store writes its projection through the core.
- **WebRTC for the phone.** The openlv session stays in the chrome renderer.

## Today

### Tokens
`signing-confirmation.js` (security audit O-7):
- **Ten kinds** (lines 74–85): `wallet-send`, `dapp-send`, `sign-message`, `sign-typed-data`, `safe-send`, `safe-message`, `intent-resend`, `intent-replace`, `intent-close`, `executor-send`. PR #1016 adds `x402-payment`. Cap edits have a twelfth, `x402-cap-update`, which 0011's APS-5 made the approvals panel's decision. It stays a token of main's, outside `fc-approval`, until X402-2 replaces it with the core's own decision (below).
- **Minting:** the chrome calls `wallet:confirm-signing` with the request as shown (`wallet-ipc.js:621–643`). Main computes `bindingFor` (`signing-confirmation.js:229–311`), digests it as SHA-256 over sorted-key JSON (`stableStringify`, 184–199), and returns 32 random bytes in hex (349).
- **Life:** two minutes of `Date.now()` (68, 93). At most 32 are outstanding, and the oldest is dropped to make room (72, 345–348).
- **Spending:** `consumeConfirmation` burns a token at its first presentation, matched or not (367–368). It refuses an expired token, another kind and another request.
- **What a token doesn't bind:** the tab or the document. A `dapp-send` binds the site, which the chrome names. 0011 adds the handle and the attested key.

### Standing rules
`authorizeSigning` (`wallet-ipc.js:307–322`) signs with a token, or with `{ autoApprove: key, webContentsId }` when `requesterBelongsTo` (239–264) finds the guest in the sending window on that key and main's own reading of the rule covers the request:
- **The account:** a rule answers only for the account the grant exposes (`permissionFor`, 124–128).
- **Messages and typed data:** the grant's `autoApprove.signing` flag (214–219). Never a `personal_sign` of exactly 32 bytes (#89, 174–176), nor typed data whose core view carries a warning (#88, 747–756).
- **Transactions:** a rule `{ to, selector, chainId, maxValue }` (`dapp-permissions.js:263–279`; with `maxValue` since #1021/#1022). A plain transfer has no selector and is never covered (`selectorOf`, `wallet-ipc.js:113–117`). **The rule caps `value`:** it stores `maxValue`, the confirmed send's `value` in wei as a decimal string, and covers the same call with at most that `value` (`ruleMaxValue`, `isTransactionAutoApproved`). A rule written before #1021 has no `maxValue` and covers only calls of 0. The same call allowed again keeps the larger of the two values. At `f19705ad` the rule had no value and covered any.
- **"Always allow" for a transaction** is added only from a confirmed `dapp-send` that carried `rememberAutoApprove` (409–421). No IPC adds one (`dapp-permissions.js:392–394`). The flag names its own key, which the chrome chooses: the token's site isn't compared with it.
- **The fee check (0005, A):** a confirmed send is judged when its token is minted (`wallet-ipc.js:623–631`), an auto-approved one as it is signed (377–380). Above 3·R, with no reference, or with one endpoint's reference, it isn't auto-approved (`fee-check.js`). A phone account picks its own fees and isn't judged. A send that names no fee of its own is refused (385).
- **D12, the pause** (`auto-approve-pause.js`): a site's dApp sends take turns until each one's payment is committed. A silent send doesn't wait for a turn, and stops while the site has a payment in Payments waiting (`wallet-ipc.js:386–401`). Records that can't be read pause it (149–151). At most 8 sends wait (38), each for at most 3 minutes (`nonce-reservations.js:25`).
- **Agent custody:** no standing rule answers for a tab the agent holds (`agent-tab-custody.js`).
- **Swarm and Radicle:** flags per site. Swarm has `publish`, `feeds`, `signing` and `messaging`, and the messaging grant. Radicle has `node` and `signing`, and its signing grant. Main reads them for the chrome's `origin` today; 0011 replaces the origin with the attested key.
- **R-6** (PR #941): a Radicle site's COB writes each need a prompt, or its `autoApprove.signing` rule, which `auto-write-limiter.js` limits per site: a burst of 5, then one more per 30 s, at most 50 in any 24 hours. Counted in memory, and forgotten on `radicle_disconnect`.

### x402
- **Caps** (`x402/permissions.js`): keyed `(origin, chainId, asset)`, stored as `origin → "chainId:asset" → { capAmount, spentAmount, createdAt, expiresAt }`, amounts as decimal strings, times as wall-clock seconds (83–89, 115–120). One window per grant, no renewal.
- **The key is the paid URL's origin** (`originKeyForUrl(details.url)`, `intercept.js:867`), not the page's. A paid resource embedded on many sites spends one cap.
- **Auto-pay:** the first `accepts[]` entry, in the seller's order, that an active cap covers is signed with no token (`findCoveringPermission` in `payment-utils.js`; `intercept.js:846–960`). Never for a tab in Agent custody, nor for a request main can't attribute to a live tab (`isUserPageId`, 868).
- **Charged at injection, not at signing:** the cap is charged when the signed header is attached (`consumeOrWithhold`, 1150–1182). Two auto-pays signed together can both pass the coverage check, and the second is withheld at injection. Its signed authorization exists all the same.
- **Manual pay:** `x402:approve` signs on a bare chrome call (`x402/ipc.js:261–340`). SEC-1 (#1016) adds the `x402-payment` token. A cap granted with a payment is written after the payment is signed (`sign-flow.js:198`).
- **Widening a cap:** `x402:update-permission` raises a cap's amount or its window on a bare chrome call (`x402/ipc.js:450–457`).
- **After 0011's APS-5:** manual pay and cap edits need the approvals panel's decision (`x402-payment`, `x402-cap-update`), still on main's word.
- **The authorization** is `@x402/evm`'s. It is EIP-3009 `TransferWithAuthorization` by default, Permit2 when the seller's `extra.assetTransferMethod` says so (`exact/client/index.js:931`). Its nonce is 32 random bytes (168–170). It reads the wall clock: `validBefore` is now plus the seller's `maxTimeoutSeconds`, and `validAfter` is `0` (V2, 177–197) or now − 600 (V1, 1001–1011). The EIP-712 domain's `name` and `version` are the seller's `extra`.

### The files
All four live in the profile's `userData`, each one JSON object keyed by permission key, written with `JSON.stringify(…, null, 2)`:

| File | Per key | How it is written |
|---|---|---|
| `dapp-permissions.json` | `{ origin, connectedAt, lastUsed, walletIndex, chainId, autoApprove: { signing, transactions: [{ to, selector, chainId }] } }` | `writeFileSync` in place (`dapp-permissions.js:62`) |
| `swarm-permissions.json` | `{ origin, connectedAt, lastUsed, autoApprove: { publish, feeds, signing, messaging }, messaging?: { grantedAt } }` | a temp file and a rename, no fsync (`swarm-permissions.js:54–64`) |
| `radicle-permissions.json` | `{ origin, connectedAt, lastUsed, signing, autoApprove: { node, signing } }` | `writeFileSync` in place (`radicle-permissions.js:50`); `lastUsed` at most once a minute (117–121) |
| `x402-permissions.json` | `{ "<chainId>:<asset>": { capAmount, spentAmount, createdAt, expiresAt } }` | `writeFileSync` in place (`x402/permissions.js:77`); expired caps dropped on load (47–58) |

- **A file that doesn't parse** loads as `{}` in all four stores (`dapp-permissions.js:48–51`; `swarm-permissions.js:38–49`), and the next write replaces it: every grant is lost. A torn in-place write does that. Swarm's temp file and rename make a torn file less likely, not impossible, since nothing is flushed.
- **A write that fails** is logged, and the change stays in memory only, until the next start: dApp (`dapp-permissions.js:59–66`), Radicle (`radicle-permissions.js:48–54`), x402 (`x402/permissions.js:75–81`). Swarm drops its cache and throws (`swarm-permissions.js:54–64`), so its change is undone.
- **Times** are `Date.now()` milliseconds, x402's seconds. **Key order** is the order in which JS inserted the keys.

### Transaction building
- **Main builds every transaction** (`transaction-service.js`): gas from `eth_estimateGas` plus 20% (51), fees from the core's quote (`resolveFeeParams`, 223–248), the type from the fee fields (`buildTransaction`, 136–176).
- **The nonce** is chosen under the account's lock: the larger of `pending` and the highest held nonce + 1 (`nonce-reservations.js:176–183`). The signed bytes are committed to `wallet-intents.jsonl` before they go anywhere (`intent-store.js`; `commitNewIntent`, `transaction-service.js:510–576`). 0005's rule holds: an intent keeps its nonce for good.

### Phone signing
- **The job** goes to the focused window's chrome (`bridge.js:93`), whose openlv session tunnels it to the phone. Main accepts the answer only from that webContents (68), marks a payment possibly sent before it leaves (`remote-signer:request-sent`, 196), and gives up after 5 minutes (50).
- **The answer:** a signature must recover to the account (`verifiedSignature` in `signer.js`). Typed data is checked with ethers' `verifyTypedData` over the dApp's own payload, not over the hash the core showed.
- **A payment** is signed and broadcast by the phone, which picks its own nonce and fees. Main checks only that the reported transaction's `from` is the account, and only if the transaction is visible at the answer (`verifyDeviceBroadcastFrom`, `transaction-service.js:185–201`). Nothing reads it again later: a phone payment has no intent record (331–340). So a chrome that relays the job can have the phone send another transaction from the same account, to another recipient or for another amount, and report its hash as the confirmed one. Only the phone's own screen shows the difference.
- **A lost answer** to a payment that may have gone is `REMOTE_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, never a failure. Only 4001, 4100 and 4200 mean "not run" (65).

## Threat model

Stage 1 trusts main (0001). To 0011's attackers (a compromised chrome renderer or sub-frame) 0012 adds bugs in main, and whoever reaches a signature through one:

| The attacker wants to | Today | After 0011 (PRQ-9, APS-5) | After APR-4, APR-7, X402-2, PHN-2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sign for a page through a main path that skips the check after the approval (the O-7 class; SEC-1's `x402:approve`) | Yes: the core signs what main asks | Yes | No: the core refuses a page's signature without a token, rule or grant it checked, a Safe owner's, a Safe executor's and a SOC included |
| Sign or grant for a page through a main path that skips the prompt | Yes | Yes: main's issuer is the only check | Yes until APR-7: the core can't see the prompt, and takes main's word. From APR-7, not for Ethereum: the core mints and grants only on the answer to its own prompt. Swarm and Radicle grants stay on main's word |
| Spend one site's or one document's token on another's request | Not another site's (a `dapp-send` binds it); another document's, yes | No: the handle and the attested key | No: checked in the core too, with the frame and the navigation |
| Use a site's rule for another account | No (`permissionFor`) | No | No, in the core |
| Widen an x402 cap without the user (chrome) | Yes: `x402:update-permission` | No: a cap edit needs the panel's decision (`x402-cap-update`, APS-5), on main's word | No: the core holds the cap, and widening needs its decision |
| Hold a signed x402 authorization beyond the cap | Yes: signed, then withheld at injection | Yes | No: the cap is charged when the core signs |
| Have the phone send another transaction of the account, reported as the confirmed one (chrome) | Yes, unless the user reads the phone | Yes | No: the core compares its fields at the answer, or once it is visible later, even after a restart, and reports it unverified until then, or unknown when it can't keep or finish the check. The phone still sends what it signed |
| Lose every grant through a torn write | Yes: three files written in place, and Swarm's unflushed | Yes | No: atomic writes |
| Keep a grant active that its file never got (a failed write) | Yes (three stores) | Yes | No: a widening that isn't written isn't applied |
| Act as main | Everything | Everything | Everything: main fills the caller context |

### What moving the policy protects against
- **Main's bugs, not main.** A signing path that forgets its check (SEC-1 found one) fails in the core instead of signing. The check sits beside the key, where every path must pass.
- **Drift.** Desktop, mobile and a later Chromium build run one copy of the rules, pinned by one set of vectors.
- **Torn files.** The core writes with 0001's atomic writes.

### What stays open
- **A compromised main** can mint any token, name any page, and call as `shell`.
- **The user's decision is main's word** until APR-7, and for Swarm and Radicle after it. The core checks that a token or a grant exists for the request, not that a prompt was shown. From APR-7 main still relays the answer to the core's own prompt, so a compromised main can still answer it.
- **A swapped phone payment still goes.** The phone signs and sends it before the core can compare it. The core stops it from being reported as the confirmed one. Only the user reading the phone stops the payment.
- **Enforcement is per caller.** The core can't see where a request came from, so it holds main to what main declares: a request declared as `page` needs a checked authorization, and one declared as `shell` doesn't. Main's dispatcher is the one sender of page requests, and a browser test pins that it never declares one as `shell` (APB-1), and the same of a page's SOC (APB-3). A Safe's signatures are the exception: under `shell` too they need a Safe approval ([A page's Safe and Swarm signatures](#a-pages-safe-and-swarm-signatures)).
- **Main's inputs.** Custody, the D12 pause, whether a document is live, and which window hosts a tab are main's state. The core takes each as main sends it, and reads a missing one as "no standing rule".
- **The chrome's wallet kinds** until APS-3 (0011), and `x402-payment` until APS-5: the chrome still asks for them.
- **0011's scripted pages** ([its open question 4](0011-page-requests.md#open-questions)): a chrome that scripts a page makes a genuine request, which a standing rule can answer.
- **Advised, not enforced:** the effects the core doesn't sign ([What the core enforces](#what-the-core-enforces)).
- **Token amounts under a rule.** Q9's value cap limits native ETH only. A contract rule's selector still covers a token's transfer or approval of any amount, within the rule's 30 days and its rate.
- **A SafeTx's other fields and a SOC's key.** Until 0016 the core checks a page's SafeTx for what its token binds: the Safe, the chain, `to`, `value` and `data`. Its operation, gas, refund and nonce fields stay main's word. Until 0015 main names a page SOC's key and identifier.
- **One seller's cap on every page** (Q11). A page that embeds a seller spends from the cap the user gave that seller on another page.
- **Token eviction:** at most 32 tokens, the oldest dropped. A chrome that mints 32 drops a page's token too. That stops a payment, and signs nothing.

## Policy in the core (Q3)

0002 left approval and policy to main until Chromium ("Policy moves with the Chromium work"). **Decided: move it now** ([Q3](#decided-questions)).
- **0011 brings the decisions into main first,** in JS (PRQ-1 to PRQ-9). That JS becomes the reference, and the core ports it with vectors, as 0002 ported ethers.
- **The core already holds each rule's inputs:** the key and the signature, the typed-data view and its warnings (#88), the fee reference (0005), and naming (0003, 0004) for a name's revalidation.
- **Mobile** links the same crates, and gets the policy with them.
- **The costs:** about fifteen new methods, a grant store the core writes, more vectors, and a time in which main and the core both run rules while the browser moves store by store.

**The alternative, as 0002 planned:** the rules stay in main's JS until the Chromium browser process exists. They are then written twice, and mobile has none until then.

## Enforced or advised (Q8)

**Decided: enforced** ([Q8](#decided-questions)), for every signature the core makes for a page. From APR-4, such a signature needs an authorization the core itself checked, for that `PageContext`:
- **a token** of a page kind, minted for that page and that request, with 0011's handle;
- **a standing rule,** which the core evaluates against its own grants for `PageContext.origin`; or
- **for a Safe's signatures, the Safe approval** its token gave, presented with that same `PageContext`, and **for a page's SOC, the page's Swarm grant** ([A page's Safe and Swarm signatures](#a-pages-safe-and-swarm-signatures)).

Anything else is refused with `not_authorized` before a key is touched. A `chrome` caller needs a token of a wallet kind for every signature.

**`shell` is outside Q8.** Q8 covers dApp signatures, the ones the core makes for a page. Main's own signatures under `shell` have no page behind them: the wallet's own sends under their wallet-kind tokens, main's own SOCs, and an x402 auto-pay until X402-2. The core checks a token when one comes, and otherwise signs on main's word, since main is trusted. A page's request never goes as `shell` (APB-1, APB-3). The one exception is a Safe's signatures: the core can't tell a page's SafeTx from the wallet's own, so it asks a Safe approval of both. The wallet's own Safe send, its owners and its executor, gets one from its wallet `safe-send` token, so it is checked though it is outside Q8.

**What that checks, and what it can't.** The core checks that a token or a rule exists, that it binds this page and this request, and that it hasn't expired or been spent. It can't see the prompt. Until APR-7, `approval.issue` mints a page kind, and `approval.grant` writes a grant, because main says the user approved. A main path that skips the prompt and calls them gets a valid token or a standing rule, and every signature it covers then passes. So enforcement protects against a check omitted after issuance, not a prompt omitted before it. Stage 1 accepts that, as it trusts main (0001). One grant is bound to a decision once the core writes the dApp store (APB-2): a transaction rule, which only a spent `dapp-send` token with its flag set writes ([The model](#the-model)).

**The end state, APR-7.** Once `fc-eip1193` runs the Ethereum dispatcher (APR-5) and the browser uses it (APB-4), the core stops admitting `page` on `approval.issue` and on `approval.grant` for the dApp store. A page-kind token is then minted only inside the core, on an approve answer to its own `approval.request`, for that prompt and 0011's handle. A connect, a signing flag or an "Always allow" is written only from the choices in that answer. Main still relays the answer, so a compromised main can still answer for the user, but a main bug that skips a prompt can no longer mint or grant. Swarm and Radicle grants stay on main's word until their dispatchers move into the core.

**Advised instead** would mean the core answers "covered" or "not", and main acts on the answer. It's cheaper, but a main path that doesn't ask still signs.

### What the core enforces

| Effect | Who signs | The core's check |
|---|---|---|
| A vault account's transaction, message or typed data, for a page | the core | enforced from APR-4 |
| A phone account's job | the phone | enforced from PHN-2: the core makes a job only from a checked authorization, and checks the answer. A payment is compared once visible, and is unverified until then |
| An x402 authorization | the core | enforced from X402-2, when main's x402 path signs only through `x402.authorize` |
| A Ledger account's signature, a Safe's Ledger owner's included | the device, through main | advised (`approval.spend`) until 0016 |
| A Safe's vault owners' signatures, and its executor's transaction | the core | enforced from APR-4, by the Safe approval a `safe-send` or `safe-message` token gives; the executor within the fee check, or with an `executor-send` token ([A page's Safe and Swarm signatures](#a-pages-safe-and-swarm-signatures)). One confirmation still collects every local owner's signature (`wallet-ipc.js:827–830`) |
| A page's Swarm SOC (feed writes, raw SOCs) | the core | enforced from APR-4, by the page's Swarm grant; main names the key and the identifier until 0015 |

| A Swarm upload or message | the Bee node | advised |

### A page's Safe and Swarm signatures

APR-4 carries a checked authorization into these too. 0016 and 0015 still decide how Safes and Swarm move into the core; the check doesn't wait for them.
- **A Safe approval.** From APR-3, `approval.spend` of a `safe-send` or `safe-message` token checks it as before, then records a Safe approval and returns its id (16 random bytes, kept like a token). It holds the token's facts. For a Safe message a standing rule answers, `approval.spend` takes `{ standing }` under `page` instead, and the core evaluates the rule for the Safe's grant itself.
- **Whose it is.** An approval from a page kind (a site's `safe-send`, a `safe-message`, or a Safe message's standing rule) records that `PageContext`. It is used only under `page`, with that same origin, frame and navigation, at each owner's signature and at the executor's. For a SafeTx that outlives its document, main sends the `PageContext` it recorded with the request. An approval from a wallet's `safe-send` is used only under `shell`.
- **The vault owners** sign through `sign.typed_data` with `{ safe: approval }`:
  - **the first,** a `SafeTx` whose domain is the token's Safe and chain and whose `to`, `value` and `data` are the token's; or a `SafeMessage` under the token's Safe whose `message` is the digest the core computed at the spend (EIP-191's for a message, the EIP-712 hash for typed data). Its signature fixes the approval's SafeTx or SafeMessage;
  - **every later one,** the other local owners at once or one when the board opens, only that same SafeTx or SafeMessage.
- **The executor** of a Safe send signs `execTransaction` to the Safe, with `value` 0 and the SafeTx the owners signed, field for field: with `{ safe: approval }` while the fee check allows it, or with an `executor-send` token when the check stops it and the user confirms the fee, as today. Its signature ends the approval.
- **Under any caller.** The core signs no `SafeTx` or `SafeMessage` typed data, and no transaction calling `execTransaction`, without a token that binds it or a Safe approval of the caller's own kind. A page's own `eth_signTypedData_v4` of a `SafeTx`, for a Safe the wallet doesn't hold, goes under `page` with its `sign-typed-data` token, as any typed data does.
- **Ending one.** `approval.end { safe }` ends an approval, and answers `{}` whether it was open, already ended or never known, so main can retry it. Every later use is refused (`unknown_token`). It replies only once no signature that presented the approval is still running: one the core checked before the end finishes first, and one checked after is refused. So nothing signs with it after the reply.
- **Narrowing first,** as 0016's approval records: main ends an approval before the step that ends its effect, so a failure after that leaves none.
  - **A SafeTx's:** before its discard, when it is superseded (the Safe's nonce moved past it), and once it is executed. The executor's signature ends it in the core as well.
  - **A SafeMessage's:** before its cancel (the user's, or its document gone, as main cancels a session today), and when the threshold's last signature is made, before main joins the signatures. Main ends it there, since the core doesn't know the threshold until 0016 reads the Safe's setup.
  - **A new `safe-send` approval for a Safe** ends that Safe's older one, as a Safe has one pending SafeTx (0016).
- **Kept in memory,** at most 64, the oldest dropped. After a restart, or once dropped, an owner that hasn't signed needs a new `safe-send` token from the board, and the executor an `executor-send` token (`safe-approval`).
- **A Ledger owner** stays advised until 0016: the device signs, not the core.
- **A page's SOC.** `swarm.sign_soc` for a page's feed write or raw SOC goes under `page`, with its `PageContext` and an authorization `{ swarm, soc }`: `swarm` is `user` or `auto`, as 0015's `approval` is, and `soc` is `feed` or `raw`, main's word for which the request is, since a SOC's parameters don't tell a feed write from a raw one. The core checks the page's Swarm grant for `PageContext.origin` itself: connected. It reads the grant from APR-3, in the file main writes until APB-3 moves it ([The files](#the-files-q10)). The feed grant (`feedGranted`) is in `swarm-feeds.json`, 0015's store, which has no vectors yet: until 0015 moves it, the feed grant is main's word, as main sends a page's SOC only after its own feed-grant check, and the core reads it from then. `auto` also needs `agent` false and the request's flag: `feeds` for a feed write, `signing` for a raw SOC. `user` is main's word that its prompt was answered, since Swarm has no token kind (0015). Main still names the key and the identifier until 0015 has the core choose them. Main's own SOCs, with no page behind them, stay `shell` (`soc-grant`).

## Crates and clocks

- **`fc-approval`** (logic, no I/O): tokens and their bindings; the grant model and the four files' codecs; the standing rules; the R-6 limiter; the fee check's judgement; Safe approvals; x402 caps and the authorization builder; phone jobs.
- **`fc-eip1193`** (logic, no I/O): the Ethereum dispatcher, a sans-I/O machine like `fc-chain`'s ([fc-eip1193](#fc-eip1193-and-approvalrequest)).
- **`freedom-cored`:** the methods, the RNG, the clocks, and the files.
- **Both crates compile for iOS and Android,** as CI requires of every logic crate (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`), and `freedom-mobile-ffi` links them later. Mobile brings its own store and prompts behind the same traits.
- **No new dependency:** `sha2`, `sha3`, `k256`, `serde_json` and `rand_core` are in the workspace. JS's key order is kept with `serde_json`'s `preserve_order`. `fc-approval` enables it itself, as `freedom-cored` does, so that its own tests and mobile builds don't rely on another crate's features.

**Time and randomness come in** (AGENTS rule 2):
- **`now` is a parameter** of every call that judges time, as in `fc-chain`. There are two clocks:
  - **The vault's clock** (`clock.rs`, #80): milliseconds that count sleep and never run back. A wall clock set forward moves it ahead. Token lives, rate limits, job timeouts and evidence ages use it. A token minted before three days asleep is expired after them. Today a wall clock set back extends a token ([Deviations](#deviations)).
  - **The wall clock,** for what is written down or signed: in seconds, x402's `createdAt` and `expiresAt` and an authorization's `validBefore`; in milliseconds, Q9's `grantedAt` and an unverified phone payment's `answeredAt`. A wall clock earlier than a cap's `createdAt`, or a rule's `grantedAt`, expires it, and one earlier than a payment's `answeredAt` ends its check as unknown ([Deviations](#deviations)).
- **Randomness** comes in as `rand_core::TryCryptoRng`, as in `fc-vault`: token bytes, x402 nonces, phone job ids. The daemon passes the OS's (`getrandom`). Tests pass a fixed one, so that a vector can pin a whole x402 payload.

## Tokens

**Who mints them** (`approval.issue`):
- **Page kinds** (`dapp-send`, `sign-message`, `sign-typed-data`, `safe-message`, and a `safe-send` with a site): only the `page` caller, with the request's `PageContext` and 0011's handle. Main's dispatcher asks on the user's approval of an open handle: it is 0011's internal issuer. The core refuses `chrome` for every page kind, as 0011 refuses it from PRQ-5. From APR-7 no caller mints them: the core mints them itself, on its own prompts ([Enforced or advised](#enforced-or-advised-q8)).
- **Wallet kinds** (`wallet-send`, a `safe-send` with no site, `intent-resend`, `intent-replace`, `intent-close`, `executor-send`): `chrome` until APS-3, then `shell` only. From APS-3 main asks as `shell` on a decision made in the approvals document. `x402-payment` follows the same rule from APS-5. `x402-cap-update` isn't a kind of the core's: main mints it on the panel's decision until X402-2.

**What each binds.** The core binds the request in the form it will sign: its own parse of the `sign.*` parameters (0002's wire format), never main's display form. What is confirmed is then what is signed.

| Kind | Binds | Spent by |
|---|---|---|

| `dapp-send` | as `wallet-send`, for the request's account; the page; the handle; whether "Always allow" was ticked | `sign.transaction` (page) |
| `sign-message` | the account; the bytes to sign; the page; the handle | `sign.message` (page) |

| `safe-send`, with a site | the Safe and its address; the SafeTx's chain, `to`, `value` and `data`; the site, as `PageContext.origin`; the page; the handle | `approval.spend` (page), which gives a [Safe approval](#a-pages-safe-and-swarm-signatures) from APR-3 |
| `safe-send`, with no site | the Safe and its address; the SafeTx's chain, `to`, `value` and `data` | `approval.spend` (chrome or shell), which gives a Safe approval from APR-3 |

| `intent-close` | the intent's id, chain, sender and nonce, and every hash, in order | `approval.spend` (advised) |

- <a id="replacing-an-intent"></a>**Replacing an intent** (amended by APR-3b). `sign.transaction` carries no intent, so an `intent-replace` is presented as `{ token, intent: { id } }`. The core compares the token's account with the signing key's account (no key is derived for it), the intent's id with the token's, and the token's transaction, its nonce, payment and fees, with the one signed. The sender's address is main's word at minting, as the reference's record is: the core reads it from the token and doesn't compare it with a derived address. A token minted without an account signs nothing; one presented without its intent, or an intent with another kind's token, is a `mismatch`.
- **"Always allow" rides in the `dapp-send` token** (`payload.remember`, a boolean, beside the token's digest): `sign.transaction` names no flag and reads the token's.
- **The page** is `PageContext`'s `origin`, `frame` and `navigation` ([PageContext](#pagecontext)). A token presented with another page is refused, so a token can't outlive its document's number, even if main's own check misses.
- **A `safe-send` with a site and one with none are two kinds to the core.** A site's is a page kind: it binds the page and the handle like the others, so it is refused for another origin, frame, navigation or handle, and under `chrome` or `shell`. A wallet's names no page, and is refused under `page`. Neither is spent as the other.
- **The nonce isn't bound,** except by `intent-replace`. Main chooses it after the confirmation, under the account's lock, until 0013 moves the journal.
- **Life and count:** two minutes of the vault's clock, at most 32 outstanding, the oldest dropped. A token is **burnt** at its first presentation, whatever the outcome, as today. `approval.discard` burns one without spending it (#1016's `discardConfirmation`).
- **A token is a bearer secret.** It is never logged, never in `Debug` output, and compared in constant time.
- **The fee check** runs at minting for `wallet-send`, `dapp-send`, `intent-replace` and `executor-send`, except for a phone account, as `wallet:confirm-signing` does today ([The fee check](#the-fee-check)).

**Parity.** The digest never leaves the core, so its bytes aren't the contract; the decisions are. For each kind, the JS reference's `bindingFor` gives the facts a payload binds, or its error. The core binds every one of those facts, as it parses them, so two payloads JS tells apart share a token in two cases only, both texts of one request that JS hashes as text and the core binds as it signs them: typed data sent as a JSON string, which the core binds parsed (`token-typed-parse`); and a message, which the core binds as its bytes, so its text, its hex in lowercase and its hex in uppercase are one message (`token-message-bytes`). It may refuse more ([Deviations](#deviations)): JS binds `chainId: true` as chain 1 (`Number(true)`); and it binds typed data sent as an object with its keys sorted, so two objects that list `types`' type names in another order share a token in JS, and not in the core ([below](#typed-data)).

### Typed data
- **Today's token binds the whole payload:** `bindingFor` hashes the typed data with `stableStringify` (`signing-confirmation.js:240–248`), and the token keeps the core's EIP-712 hash beside it for `expected_hash` (343).
- **The hash alone isn't enough.** EIP-712 ignores a message field the types don't declare, and 0002's view lists a top-level one as sent but not signed. Two requests can share a hash and differ in what the user was shown.
- **So the token binds three things:**
  - the EIP-712 hash;
  - a SHA-256 over the request's canonical JSON as `sign.typed_data` receives it: `typed_data` with every object's keys sorted and every member kept, undeclared ones at any depth included, then `primary_type` and `chain_id`;
  - a SHA-256 of the view the core returned at minting. `sign.typed_data` builds the view again, as 0002 has it return one, and compares.
- **Why the view as well.** The view is built from those inputs, but it lists `types`' type names in the order the request gives them, as ethers' `getPayload` does (0002; `fc-evm`'s `eip712.rs`), and the sorted JSON doesn't keep that order. With its own digest, the view the user was shown is the view of what is signed, byte for byte.
- **What one token covers.** Two texts of one request that differ only in spacing, in key order (but for `types`' list of type names) or in how a string is escaped parse to the same canonical JSON and the same view. They share a token, as they share the hash and the signature (`token-typed-parse`). Two that list `types`' type names in another order share the hash and the signature but not the view, so they get two tokens.

## Grants and standing rules

### The model
- **A grant** is a site's entry in one of the four stores, under its permission key: 0011's `key`, which is `PageContext.origin`. x402's key is the paid URL's origin, as today ([x402](#x402)).
- **The core writes a grant only on a decision, and in Stage 1 the decision is main's word:** a connect's account and chain, a signing flag, a Swarm or Radicle tier the user approved. They come through `approval.grant` from the `page` caller, for its own key. The core can't tell that a prompt was shown ([Enforced or advised](#enforced-or-advised-q8)). From APR-7 the dApp store's grants come only from the core's own prompts.
- **A transaction rule comes only with a confirmed send.** From APB-2, when the dApp file has moved ([The files](#the-files-q10)): when `sign.transaction` spends a `dapp-send` token whose "Always allow" flag is set, the core writes the rule for the token's page, after the signature, if that page's grant exposes the account, and the reply says whether it did, as `autoApproveAdded` does today. `approval.grant` refuses a transaction rule. Today the rule is written once main has recorded the send (`wallet-ipc.js:409–421`); the core writes it as it signs (`token-remember`).
  - **Before APB-2** (APB-1: tokens go through the core, and main still writes the dApp file), the flag doesn't make `sign.transaction` write a rule. The core checks the token and signs as above, writes nothing, and its reply says no rule was written because the file hasn't moved (`not_moved`); the signature stands. Main writes today's rule after the core's signature, once it has recorded the send, as it does today.
- **An x402 cap** comes with its payment, through `x402.authorize`.
- **The chrome** may read and narrow (revoke, turn a rule off), as 0011 allows. It can't widen.
- **Main's own writes** come as `shell`: a Swarm manifest's projection (`permission-manifests.js`) until 0015, and an account's deletion (`revokePermissionsForWalletIndex`). Each comes through the core from its file's move step; until then main makes it itself, as today.
- **Events:** each change sends `approval.changed { store, key }`, so that main can send `accountsChanged` and Swarm's `connect` and `disconnect` to that key's documents (0011's events).

### The rules
Each is today's, evaluated in the core for `PageContext.origin` and pinned by vectors from the JS:

| Request | A standing rule answers when |
|---|---|
| `personal_sign` | the grant exposes the account, its `autoApprove.signing` is on, and the message isn't exactly 32 bytes |
| `eth_signTypedData_v4` | as for `personal_sign`, and the core's view carries no warning |
| `eth_sendTransaction` | the grant exposes the account; a rule matches `to`, the first four bytes of `data` and the chain; the site isn't paused (D12); the fee check allows it; the send names its own fees |
| a Safe message | as a message or typed data, for the Safe's grant; it gives a Safe approval |
| a Swarm publish, feed, signature or message | the grant's flag for that type, and the messaging grant for messages; enforced for a page's SOC, which the core signs, and advised for the rest |
| a Radicle seed or unseed; a COB write | `autoApprove.node`; `autoApprove.signing` within the R-6 limit (advised) |
| an x402 charge | [x402](#x402) |

- **No rule answers in custody.** `PageContext` carries `agent` from APR-3 ([PageContext](#pagecontext)). Main reads it live as it sends each request, as 0011's custody rules require, and a request without it gets no standing rule.
- **The D12 pause stays main's.** Its turns end when the intent journal commits, and the journal is main's. A standing authorization carries `paused`, which main reads in the send's own turn. A missing one reads as paused.
- **R-6 moves as it is:** per key, a burst of 5, one per 30 s, 50 in 24 hours, on the vault's clock, in memory, forgotten on disconnect.

### Limits on "Always allow" (Q9)
"Always allow" has no limits today but R-6's. **Decided: three limits** ([Q9](#decided-questions)). They cover the dApp store's two rules, the signing flag and the transaction rules. Swarm's flags and Radicle's keep today's rules, R-6 included.
- **Expiry: 30 days.** A rule stores `grantedAt`, in wall-clock milliseconds like the file's other times, and answers while `grantedAt ≤ now < grantedAt + 2,592,000,000`. At exactly 30 days it is expired, and a wall clock earlier than `grantedAt` expires it, as for x402. Use doesn't renew it. A new "Always allow" for the same rule (the signing flag again, or a send to the same `to`, selector and chain) replaces it, with a new `grantedAt`. A restart changes nothing, since the time is in the file.
- **Rate: R-6's limiter, as it is.** A burst of 5, refilled continuously at one per 30 s, and at most 50 in any 24 hours: a use counted at `t` stops counting at exactly `t` + 24 h. On the vault's clock.
  - **One bucket per key and request class:** one for the key's message and typed-data signatures, one for its transactions, which all its transaction rules share. A site with many rules gets no more than a site with one.
  - **In memory, as R-6:** a restart starts every bucket full and empty, and a revoked grant (a disconnect) forgets its buckets. A new grant needs the user's prompts again.
  - **Over the limit, the prompt:** the request isn't refused, it is asked, as in R-6, and one the user approves there isn't counted.
- **Value: no more than the send the rule came from.** A transaction rule stores `maxValue`, the confirmed send's `value` in wei as a decimal string, and covers a call whose `value` is at most that. Per call, not summed. A rule from a send of 0 covers only calls of 0. **It caps native ETH only.** A token's transfer or approval carries its amount in `data`, with `value` 0, and the rule's selector covers it for any amount, as today.
- **Stored where an older build doesn't look:** under `autoApprove.limited`, as `{ signing: { grantedAt } | null, transactions: [{ to, selector, chainId, maxValue, grantedAt }] }`. An older build doesn't read it ([The files](#the-files-q10)).
- **Today's rules** (`autoApprove.signing`, `autoApprove.transactions`) carry no time, so from APB-2, when the core writes the dApp file, it answers none of them. Each such site prompts once after that update. Before APB-2 only main writes the file and no limited rule exists in it, so the core answers today's rules there as main does ([The files](#the-files-q10)).
- **Against today's transaction rule,** which already caps `value` with its `maxValue`, the limited rule differs in three ways: an older build doesn't see it; it expires after 30 days and is rate-limited; and a new "Always allow" for the same call replaces its value, where today's keeps the larger. So a limited rule never covers more `value` than the last send the user confirmed for it.
- **A limited rule replaces today's in one write.** Ticking "Always allow" at that prompt writes the limited rule and clears the old rule it covers, in the same atomic write of `dapp-permissions.json`:
  - **The signing flag:** `limited.signing` is set, and `autoApprove.signing` becomes `false`.
  - **A transaction rule:** the limited rule is added, and each entry of `autoApprove.transactions` that today's `isTransactionAutoApproved` matches for its `to`, selector and chain is removed: the same `to` and selector in lowercase, and the same `chainId` (`dapp-permissions.js:263–279`). It is removed whatever its `maxValue`: kept, an old rule with a larger one would give an older build more than the limited rule that replaces it allows.
  - **So a rollback brings back no unlimited rule** for it. An older build finds neither rule and prompts, as Q10 promises of every new limit ([The files](#the-files-q10)).
  - **One change, written once.** A write that fails before the rename applies neither half: the file keeps the old rule, which the new build doesn't answer, and the limited rule isn't active. After a failed directory flush both halves are in place (`durable: false`), and a crash can at most bring back the file from before, with both undone.
- **An old rule never replaced** stays in the file. The new build doesn't answer it, and an older build answers it as the user left it there: a rollback gives the older build no authority it didn't have.
- **Turning a rule off, or a revoke,** clears the limited rule and the old one it covers, in one write.

### The fee check
0005's check A moves with the tokens and the rules: `fee-check.js`'s `referenceFee` and `judgeFees`, ported with vectors.
- **The evidence is the core's own:** the newest `chain.fee_reference` reply it served for that chain, aged from when it admitted the request, and at most 60 s old. Newest is the order the requests were admitted in, not their times, so an older request answered late never replaces a newer one admitted in the same millisecond. Main fetches one before the prompt, as it does today. A figure main sends is never used.
- **At minting:** a send above the threshold gets no token without `fee_ack`, and the reply carries the verdict for the screen. A phone account's send (`approval.issue`'s `phone: true`) isn't judged: the phone picks its own fees.
- **Under a standing rule:** a send with no reference, with one endpoint's reference, or with a fee above the threshold isn't covered.

## The files (Q10)

**Decided: older builds still read the files in Stage 1** ([Q10](#decided-questions)), **so the core takes the four over in place.**
- **Where:** `core.hello` gains `grant_root`, the profile's `userData` as an absolute path, beside `node_roots`. The core opens the four files there under 0001's reading rules: a regular file the user owns, no symlink, a size bound. On Windows the core doesn't check a file's owner yet: the default root's per-user `%APPDATA%` ACL protects it, as 0001 has it. Split roots break that: `--profile-dir` can put `grant_root` where another account creates files while `FREEDOM_IDENTITY_DATA` keeps the vault protected, so a file planted there would answer `{ standing }`. **An owner check on Windows is required before APB-1,** the first step that sends `{ standing }` ([freedom-core#360](https://github.com/solardev-xyz/freedom-core/issues/360)): a file another account owns reads as an empty store, as on Unix. 0001's owner-only DACL follow-up covers a file the user owns that another account may edit.
  - **Required from APR-3.** Main starts sending `grant_root` in the same browser change that pins the first APR-3 core ([Migration](#migration)), so no core that requires it is ever paired with a main that doesn't send it, and the core has no mode without it. Once that core is pinned, a hello without the field, or with a path that isn't absolute, is a protocol error: the core answers `invalid_argument` and exits, as for any malformed hello. A core before APR-3 ignores the field.
- **The format** is today's, byte for byte: the same keys, `JSON.stringify(…, null, 2)`'s text, and JS's key order (insertion order, as each store's operations leave it). Vectors pin the text after each operation.
- **Writes** are 0001's single-file writes: a temp file, fsync, a rename, the directory flushed, mode `0600`. Each file has one writer: main until the file's move step, the core from it (below). From then main reads and writes it only through the core.
- **One writer per file, moved one at a time.** From APR-3 the core reads all four files, but it writes a file only from that file's move step: `dapp-permissions.json` from APB-2, `swarm-permissions.json` and `radicle-permissions.json` from APB-3, `x402-permissions.json` from X402-2. Main names the moved files in `core.hello`'s `grant_moved` (`dapp`, `swarm`, `radicle`, `x402`), and each step adds its file in the same browser change that stops main's writes to it. Before a file's move:
  - **Main is its only writer,** as today. The core changes nothing in it: each method that would write it answers `not_moved` ([API](#api)), and nothing is written, renamed or removed. So the core sends no `approval.changed` for that store, and main sends its events as today.
  - **Both sides write by a temp file and a rename,** so a read never sees a torn file. Main's dApp, Radicle and x402 writes, in place today, become renames in the browser change that pins APR-3 ([Migration](#migration)).
  - **The core reads the file for its checks:** a dApp's standing rules and the account its grant exposes, a page's Swarm grant for its SOC, `approval.check` and `approval.grants`. It reads what main last renamed into place, so a grant main writes answers at the core's next check, and one main removes stops answering.
  - **A missing file is an empty store:** no grant, so no standing rule. `approval.check` answers not covered and main prompts; a `{ standing }` authorization is refused `no_rule`. It is never a storage or configuration error.
  - **An unreadable file, or one past a bound,** reads as an empty store too, and stays where it is: renaming it aside is a write, which waits for the move. Main reads it as it does today.
- **Bounds** (proposed): 4 MiB (4,194,304 bytes) per file, 4,096 keys per store, 256 transaction rules per key, `autoApprove.transactions` and `autoApprove.limited.transactions` counted together. A file exactly at each bound is read and written. A write that would pass one is refused (`invalid_argument`), and the store is unchanged. A file past one is read as unreadable.
- **A failed write** of the temp file, its fsync or the rename leaves the file as it was, and the change is reported as failed:
  - **A widening** (a new grant, rule or tier, a higher cap, a cap's charge given back) isn't applied in memory either, so nothing is active that the file lacks.
  - **A narrowing** (a revoke, a rule turned off, a cap lowered) is applied in memory, since keeping what the user removed is the worse failure, and is reported as not saved: the store's next write carries it, but a restart before then reads the old file.
  - **An x402 charge** is written before the core signs, and a failed write refuses the payment with nothing signed.
  - **A failed directory flush** after the rename is 0001's `durable: false`: the change is in place, and reported as applied but not durable.
- **An unreadable file** that has moved is renamed aside once (`<name>.unreadable`), and the store starts empty, so every site asks again. Today the next write overwrites it. Before its move it stays where it is (above).
- **Older builds read the files as they are.** A grant revoked in the new build stays revoked after a rollback.
- **New fields fail safe.** Anything that limits a rule (Q9's expiry or value cap; its rate is kept in memory) is written where an older build doesn't look, such as `autoApprove.limited`, and the old rule a limited one replaces is cleared in the same write ([Q9](#limits-on-always-allow-q9)). So an older build sees no rule for it and prompts. A grant never gains authority in an older build.
- **What an older build changes** reaches only the fields it knows. A disconnect there removes the site's entry, and a new connect replaces it, `limited` with it in both cases. A rule ticked again and then turned off there changes only the old rule: back in the new build, a limited rule given before the rollback still answers, until it expires or is turned off.

**The overlap with 0013.** 0013 decides which stores the core owns in the end (Q14), where they live (`<profile>/freedom/`), and whether that move is one-way (Q12). 0012 moves who writes the files, not where they are. `fc-approval`'s store trait is what 0013 relocates. The intent journal and the nonces are 0013's.

## Transaction building

**Proposed: from APR-5 the core prepares a page's send. Main keeps the nonce, the journal and the broadcast in Stage 1,** as 0002 and 0005 set.

| Step | Until APR-5 | From APR-5: a page's `eth_sendTransaction` | The wallet's own sends |
|---|---|---|---|
| The gas limit: `eth_estimateGas` plus 20% | main | the core, through its own chain machine | main |
| The fees, from the core's quote, and the type they imply | main | the core | main |
| What the prompt shows and the token binds | main's prepared send | the core's | main's |
| The nonce, under the account's lock | main | main | main |
| The final transaction | main | main: the prepared fields unchanged, and the nonce | main |
| The signature (`sign.transaction`, with the token) | the core | the core | the core |
| The journal (`wallet-intents.jsonl`), then the broadcast | main | main | main |

- **Why not the nonce now:** the nonce is chosen from the journal's held nonces, and the journal's rules (a commit before any byte leaves, voiding, retiring) rest on its crash safety. Moving the nonce without the journal moves nothing: the core would take the larger of two numbers main supplies. The journal is a store, so 0013 moves it (Q14).
- **What moves first is the binding.** From APR-4 the core signs only the fields a token or a rule authorized: every `sign.transaction` field but the nonce, so the type, the chain, `to`, `value`, `data`, the gas limit and the fee fields. A main bug between the confirmation and the signature can change only the nonce.
- **`fc-eip1193`** prepares a page's send itself, since the estimate and the quote are reads the core already serves. Its result `{ send }` carries the prepared transaction without a nonce, and its token. Main adds the nonce and passes the rest to `sign.transaction` unchanged. Any other change is a mismatch, and nothing is signed.
- **A phone account** prepares nothing: the phone picks its own gas, fees and nonce, as today ([Phone jobs](#phone-jobs)).
- **APR-6 is therefore deferred.** The nonce, the journal and the broadcast move with 0013's intent store, if the user agrees there.

A failed write before the rename is `storage`, with `data: { store, applied }`: `applied` is true for a narrowing, active in memory and not saved, and false for a widening, not applied. A write past a bound is `invalid_argument`.
- **APR-3 checks an authorization when one is present,** and still signs without one, as today. **APR-4 requires it.** So the browser can pass authorizations one release before the core insists.
- **Events:** `approval.changed { store, key }`, from the core to main, for each grant written or revoked; `phone.checked { job, outcome }` for a phone payment compared or ended after its answer, and `phone.lost {}` once, at a start that found `phone-payments.json` unreadable ([Unverified payments](#unverified-payments)).
- **The order of work** is 0002's: all public work first, the authorization check included, then the key. A token presented while the vault is locked is still burnt, as today.

## fc-eip1193 and approval.request

APR-5 moves 0011's Ethereum dispatcher (`src/main/page/`, in JS) into the core.
- **`eip1193.request { method, params }`** (page caller): the core runs the request. Reads go to its own chain machine (0005). `eth_accounts` and the connect methods read its grants. Signatures go through its own tokens and rules. Its result is `{ result }`; or `{ error }`, with the EIP-1193 code main returns today; or `{ send }`, the prepared transaction without its nonce, and its token, for main to add the nonce, have it signed, record it and broadcast it ([Transaction building](#transaction-building)).
- **`approval.request`** is a core-to-main request, like 0004's `shell.*`: `{ request, prompt, page, kind, view, choices }` → `{ prompt, handle, approve, choices }`, or `{ prompt, closed }`, or `{ prompt, busy }`.
  - **`request`** is the 0001 id of the `eip1193.request` it serves, so that main opens the prompt on that request's record. `page` alone can't tell two requests of one document apart.
  - **`prompt`** is 16 random bytes the core makes for each prompt, and never reuses. It is the core's identity for the prompt.
  - **Main opens a 0011 handle for it,** on that request's record, and answers only with the decision made on that handle, echoing `prompt` and the handle. It answers `closed` when that handle closes (cancelled, its document gone, its window closed), and `busy` when the window already has a wallet prompt open (the page gets `-32002`).
  - **The core checks the answer:** its `prompt` must be the one sent on that core-to-main request, and its handle one the core hasn't seen for another prompt. Otherwise the request fails, with nothing signed and nothing granted.
  - **An approve** mints the page-kind token inside the core, bound to the page, the handle and the prompt, and the core spends it for that request only. It leaves the core only in a `{ device }` result (below). A grant the prompt offers (a connect's account and chain, an "Always allow") is written from the answer's `choices`, for that page.
  - **The view:** the core builds `view` to 0011's closed schema. Main adds its own fields (the tab, the site's label) and shows it in the approvals document.
- **`approval.recheck`** is another: `{ request, page }` → `{ live, agent }`, sent just before an effect a standing rule answered, as 0011 reads custody and the document again.
- **Names over IPNS:** with `transport_key`, the core resolves the name itself before each use of its grant, by 0011's table of methods, with its own naming.
- **Deadlines:** a prompt has none (0011). `eip1193.request` gets a new deadline class: no overall deadline while one of its own `approval.request`s is open, and each I/O step keeps its own. Main's hang watchdog skips a request while main is answering one of its prompts.
- **Ledger and Safe accounts** (until 0016): their signing methods come back to main as `{ device }`, with the approved request and its token.
- **Vectors:** `vectors/eip1193.json`, recorded from 0011's JS dispatcher: each request, each effect and its answer, and the result, as `naming-replay.json` records naming.

## x402

X402-1 moves the caps, the `x402-payment` token and the authorization into the core. Main keeps the interception (`webRequest`), the 307 and the header.
- **`x402.authorize { account, detection: { tab, id, url }, requirements, accept_index, grant?, authorization }`** (shell). The `authorization` is `{ token }`, an `x402-payment` token, or `{ cap: { agent, attributable } }`:
  - **Under a cap:** the first entry, in the seller's order, that an active cap covers, as `findCoveringPermission` picks it. None in custody, and none for a request main couldn't attribute.
  - **With a token:** exactly the entry and the grant it binds.
  - **The authorization** is built by the core from that entry, byte for byte as `@x402/evm` 2.28.0 builds it (EIP-3009 for V1 and V2, and Permit2), with the nonce from the RNG and `validBefore` from the wall clock, then signed inline.
  - **The cap is charged when the core signs,** in the same turn. A grant that comes with a payment is written after its signature, as today.
  - The reply is the payment payload and a reservation id.
- **`x402.settle { reservation, outcome }`:** `withheld` gives the amount back, since main dropped the header; `sent` keeps it.
- **Widening a cap** (a higher amount, a longer window) needs a decision, as a new grant does. The activity pane can only narrow one (X402-2).
- **The key stays the paid URL's origin** ([Q11](#decided-questions), decided), main's word from `webRequest`. So unrelated pages that embed one seller share its cap, as today. The fixes are [Deviations](#deviations)' `x402-*` rows: a cap charged when the core signs, expired under a clock set back, its asset in lowercase, and widened only on a decision.

## Phone jobs

PHN-1 makes the core the job's authority. The openlv session stays in the chrome, and main relays.
- **`phone.job { account, method, payload, authorization, target }`:** the core checks the authorization as `sign.*` would, then records a job:
  - its id (16 random bytes), the account's index and address;
  - the method and what it binds: the message bytes; the typed data's confirmed hash and its view; a payment's chain, `to`, `value` and `data`;
  - the authorization it came from (its kind, site and handle);
  - `target`, the id of the chrome's webContents that main chose to send it to (the focused window's, `bridge.js:93`), fixed before the job goes anywhere;
  - five minutes of the vault's clock;
  - for a payment, one of the 256 record slots ([Unverified payments](#unverified-payments)), taken before the reply. With none free, or with a site key over 2,048 bytes as written in the record, the job is refused (`busy`, `invalid_argument`), and nothing goes to the phone.

  The reply is the JSON-RPC request to relay. For typed data it carries the core's view, so the phone signs what the user saw ([Deviations](#deviations)).
- **The sender.** `fc-protocol`'s `Context` names no chrome, so `phone.sent` and `phone.result` carry `sender`: the id of the webContents whose IPC message brought them, which main fills from `event.sender.id` and never takes from the message. The core refuses one whose `sender` isn't the job's `target` (`wrong_sender`), and the job stays open for its own chrome, as `jobOf` ignores such a message today (`bridge.js:68`). Main keeps its own check as well.
- **`phone.sent { job, sender }`** marks the job possibly sent, in one turn, as `remote-signer:request-sent` does. It is refused for a settled job.
- **`phone.result { job, sender, result | error }`** is accepted once:
  - **A signature** must be 65 bytes and recover to the account over the bound digest (EIP-191 over the bytes, or the confirmed EIP-712 hash). Otherwise it is refused (`wrong_account`, `bad_response`).
  - **A payment's hash:** the core reads the transaction on the job's chain (`eth_getTransactionByHash`, 0005) and compares its `from`, `to`, `value`, `data` and chain id with the job's. A transaction with no chain id (pre-EIP-155) differs.
    - **Visible and the same:** `{ outcome: "verified", hash }`.
    - **Visible and different:** refused (`wrong_transaction`), with the transaction's fields, so that main shows what the phone sent.
    - **Not visible, or the read failed:** `{ outcome: "unverified", hash }`, once the core has saved a record of it ([Unverified payments](#unverified-payments)). If the save fails, `{ outcome: "unknown", hash, durable: false }`.
  - **An error** is mapped as today. After `phone.sent`, a payment's lost or unrecognised answer is unknown. Only 4001, 4100 and 4200 mean "not run".
- **Main still** chooses the window, relays the job, and tells the core which webContents answered. A chrome can drop or delay a job. It can't have a swapped payment reported as the confirmed one, but the phone still sends what it signed.

### Unverified payments
- **The record:** the job's id, the hash, the account, the chain, `to`, `value`, the length of `data` and its SHA-256 (never `data` itself), the authorization's kind and site, and `answeredAt`, the wall-clock time of the answer in milliseconds. It goes into `phone-payments.json`, a new file beside the four (older builds don't read it; 0013 may move it), by 0001's atomic write, before `phone.result` replies. Comparing a transaction's `data` by its length and SHA-256 is as strict as comparing the bytes: a different `data` would need a SHA-256 collision.
- **Bounds:** at most 256 records, each at most 4,000 bytes as written, and the file at most 1 MiB (1,048,576 bytes), which 256 records at their bound fit with room to spare. Every field but the site has a fixed largest size, so a site key of at most 2,048 bytes as written, escapes included, keeps a record within its bound.
- **Reserved at `phone.job`.** A payment's job takes one of the 256 slots before its reply leaves the core, so its record has room before the phone can have sent anything. Open payment jobs and records share the slots. A slot comes back when its job ends without a record (an error, an expiry, or a transaction visible at the answer, the same or not), and when its record is dropped.
- **A failed save.** The phone may have sent the payment by then, so the core never answers with an error that invites a retry, and never says it wasn't sent. If any stage of the write fails (the temp file's write, its fsync, the rename, the directory flush):
  - `phone.result` answers `{ outcome: "unknown", hash, durable: false }`, and the job is settled: a second result for it is refused.
  - The record stays in memory, in its job's slot, and is checked as a saved one is, so a `phone.checked` can still follow. The file's next write carries it.
  - A restart before then loses it, and the payment stays unknown. After a failed directory flush the file has the record, but a crash can undo it.
- **Read** under 0001's rules: a regular file the user owns, opened through no symlink, and no more than its bound read. A file that fails them, or that doesn't parse, or holds more than 256 records or a record over 4,000 bytes, is renamed aside (`phone-payments.json.unreadable`). The core then starts with no records and sends `phone.lost`, so that main shows each phone payment it still lists as not yet checked as unknown.
- **Rechecked** with `eth_getTransactionByHash` after 5 s, then at doubling intervals up to every 10 minutes, and at each start of the core, so that a restart loses none.
- **The age comes first.** At each recheck and at each start, before any chain read, a record waits only while `answeredAt ≤ now < answeredAt + 86,400,000` (24 hours) on the wall clock. Otherwise it ends as `unknown` for good, reported once, and is dropped. A clock moved either way ends a check as unknown, never as verified. No record outlives a day of the wall clock, or a clock set back, so stale records can't hold the 256 slots and refuse every later phone payment.
- **Once it is visible,** the core compares the same fields as at the answer, `data` by its length and SHA-256, and sends `phone.checked { job, outcome }`: `verified`, or `mismatch` with the transaction's fields. Main shows a mismatch as another transaction sent from the phone, never as the confirmed one. The record is then dropped. If that write fails, it is dropped in memory and the next write carries it; a restart before then finds it again and reports it once more.
- **Until then the outcome is unverified.** The page gets the hash, as today: the phone sent that transaction, and the hash is how the page follows it. The wallet shows the payment as sent from the phone and not yet checked, never as confirmed.

## Options weighed

- **Advise only** (Q8's alternative): one call to ask, and no change to `sign.*`. A path that doesn't ask still signs.
- **"Always allow" as it is** (Q9's alternative): no end, no rate and any amount. No site asks again, but a rule given once answers for good, as often as the site likes, for any `value`.
- **New files in the core's own directory,** instead of today's: older builds would read stale grants, and a grant revoked since would come back on a rollback. Only writing both, or a one-way move, avoids that, and 0013 weighs the one-way move.
- **Binding JS's digest byte for byte** (`stableStringify`): it never leaves the process, so only the decision matters, and binding the core's own parse binds what it signs.
- **Moving the nonce now:** without the journal it moves nothing ([Transaction building](#transaction-building)).
- **0011's handle passed into `eip1193.request`,** instead of a prompt id the core makes. Main would have to open a handle before it knows a prompt is needed, and a request that needs two prompts (an unlock, then the send) would need two, so 0011's rule that a prompt is a handle would change. The core's own id answers the same question, which prompt a decision is for, and the core checks it against what it sent. The handle is still bound into the token.
- **Grants on main's word for good,** with no APR-7: simpler, but a main path that skips a prompt could still mint a token or write a grant after the dispatcher has moved into the core.
- **Custody state kept in the core,** told by events: an event can arrive after the request it should have stopped. Read with each request, it can't.
- **The core fetching its own fee reference** for each check: a second way into the chain machine, for evidence main already fetches before every prompt.
- **WebRTC in the core for the phone:** a large new surface. Checking the answer gives the same assurance about what was signed.
- **A cap per page and seller** (Q11's alternative): each page that embeds a seller would need its own cap, so one page couldn't spend what the user gave that seller on another. It costs a prompt and a cap for every embedding page.

2. **Q8, enforced:** Should the core refuse any dApp signature without an approval or an "Always allow" it checked itself? Until the core runs the Ethereum dispatcher, it takes main's word that you approved, so this catches a check main forgets after your approval, not a prompt main forgets to show.
   - **Decided (the user, 2026-11-12): enforced,** for every signature the core makes for a page: a vault account's, a Safe's vault owners' and executor's, and a Swarm SOC's. Once the core runs the Ethereum dispatcher, it asks for those prompts itself (APR-7). Where the core doesn't make the signature, it advises. Main's own `shell` signatures, with no page behind them, are outside it ([Enforced or advised](#enforced-or-advised-q8)).
3. **Q9, "Always allow":** Keep a site's "Always allow" as it is today, with no end, no rate and any amount, or add three limits? (a) A rule ends 30 days after you gave it. (b) A site's automatic signatures, and separately its automatic sends, go at most 5 at once, then one per 30 seconds and 50 a day, and past that you're asked. (c) A contract rule covers no more ETH than the send you allowed it from. This doesn't limit token amounts, which travel inside the call. With any of them, sites you have already allowed ask you once more. When you allow one again, its old unlimited rule is removed, so an older version of the browser asks too; a site you don't allow again keeps its old rule there.
   - **Decided (the user, 2026-11-12): all three.** (a) A rule ends 30 days after it was given. (b) A site's automatic signatures, and separately its automatic sends, go at most 5 at once, then one per 30 seconds and 50 a day, and past that the user is asked. (c) A contract rule covers no more ETH than the send it was allowed from. Sites already allowed ask once more. Allowing one again removes its old unlimited rule, so an older build asks too; a site not allowed again keeps its old rule there ([Limits on "Always allow"](#limits-on-always-allow-q9)).
4. **Q10, rollback:** Must older builds still read the grant files after the move (rollback)? They would read what you left: a grant revoked in the new build stays revoked, and a rule you allowed again under Q9's limits asks again there.
   - **Decided (the user, 2026-11-13): yes, in Stage 1.** Older builds still read the grant files after the move: a grant revoked in the new build stays revoked, and a rule allowed again under Q9's limits asks again there ([The files](#the-files-q10)).
5. **Q11, x402:** An x402 spending cap belongs to the seller's site (the paid link's origin), not to the page you're on, so every page that embeds the same seller spends from one cap. Keep that, or give each cap to the pair of the page you're on and the seller, so that each embedding page needs its own?
   - **Decided (the user, 2026-11-13): the seller-only cap,** keyed on the paid link's origin, with the fixes in [Deviations](#deviations): `x402-at-sign`, `x402-clock-back`, `x402-asset-case` and `x402-widen` ([x402](#x402)).
